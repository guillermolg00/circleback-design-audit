Circleback already has a solid foundation: restrained interface type (12–15px), cards with a 12px radius and a 0.5px border, a very regular sidebar, and patterns borrowed from Linear, such as ⌘K, a shortcuts panel, multi-select, and a well-organized Settings. What’s missing is **closing the system**: too many values coexist for the same thing, and the same component is built differently from screen to screen.

## What matters most

- **Errors are dead ends.** In the meetings list, 5 of 8 visible rows are errors whose only action is *Delete*. A failed meeting’s page is left empty. → [Dead-end errors](/docs/ux/errors)
- **The same object has three different menus.** The “…” menu, the right-click menu, and the shared version offer different actions, and use “Delete” or “Remove” for the same action. → [Menus per object](/docs/ux/menus)
- **Menus don’t share measurements:** items of 31, 36, 40, and 58px, radii of 6 and 15px, three key styles, and four search field styles. → [Menu and popover](/docs/components/menu)
- **List rows are built differently:** the hover bleeds 34px in Meetings and 8px in People and Companies. Checkboxes come in three versions. → [List row](/docs/components/list-row)
- **Settings has two templates** and, within Calendar, the two cards use different heights, text sizes, and dividers. → [Settings](/docs/components/settings)
- **The proposal** reduces the system to 5 radii, 4 control heights, 2 borders, 3 text grays, and 4 icon sizes, defines one rule per component, and orders 15 changes into three stages. → [Tokens](/docs/proposal/tokens) · [Roadmap](/docs/proposal/roadmap)
